# Search Everywhere Audit Method

## Audit question

A Search Everywhere audit asks:

> Where does this audience search while trying to understand, compare, validate, and act, and is the client visible and convincing at each moment?

The audit does not assume that every client needs every platform. Platform importance is based on audience behavior, decision risk, business model, geography, content format, and measurable opportunity.

## Step 1: Frame the business decision

Document:

- Primary conversion
- Revenue or lead model
- Priority products or services
- Geographic constraints
- Repeat-purchase or retention behavior
- Compliance constraints
- Teams available for execution

## Step 2: Define audience segments and need states

Avoid demographic-only personas. Define what creates the search and what the person must resolve.

Examples:

**Breezy Golf**

- Wants a distinctive golf polo but needs fit confidence.
- Knows the creator brand but wants independent proof.
- Needs hot-weather performance or big-and-tall options.
- Is comparing Breezy with Rhoback, Peter Millar, or other golf apparel brands.

**Kanner & Pintaluga**

- Has an underpaid or denied claim and does not know the next step.
- Is deciding whether a lawyer is necessary.
- Needs a legitimate nearby office.
- Wants to understand fees, timing, and firm credibility before contact.

## Step 3: Map the decision journey

Use seven stages:

1. Trigger
2. Define
3. Generate options
4. Compare
5. Validate
6. Act
7. Return or advocate

For each stage, record:

- Audience question
- Search surface
- Query or prompt
- Result type
- Evidence needed
- Likely transition to the next surface
- Failure risk

## Step 4: Build a platform hypothesis

Rate likely relevance before research.

| Platform type | Common role |
|---|---|
| Google organic | Explicit category, service, policy, comparison, and informational demand |
| Maps and local | Proximity, directions, office legitimacy, hours, calls, reviews |
| AI answers | Need definition, synthesis, recommendation, citation, fact retrieval |
| YouTube | Demonstration, walkthrough, first-hand proof, process explanation |
| TikTok and Instagram | Visual discovery, creator-led answers, trend and style language |
| Reddit and forums | Peer language, objections, tradeoffs, category validation |
| Reviews and directories | Reputation, recency, specificity, credentials, local proof |
| Shopping and marketplaces | Product comparison, price, availability, attributes, ratings |
| Brand website | Canonical facts, detail, conversion, owned proof, retention |

The hypothesis controls research time. It is not the final priority.

## Step 5: Create the query library

Include more than keywords. Capture the language a person may type, speak, or ask.

Query categories:

- Category
- Problem or symptom
- Product or service
- Comparison
- “Worth it” or legitimacy
- Reviews
- Cost and fees
- Location
- Process
- Safety or risk
- Policy
- Alternatives
- Branded validation
- Post-purchase or post-contact

## Step 6: Observe the live environment

For each query or prompt, record:

- Date and location
- Device or result context
- Platform
- Result types
- Client presence
- Competitor presence
- Third-party sources
- Citations
- Reviews or comments
- Visual formats
- Unanswered questions
- Conversion path

Use screenshots or direct URLs where policy allows.

## Step 7: Evaluate two views

### Human view

What does the person experience?

- Relevance
- Clarity
- Convenience
- Proof
- Credibility
- Consensus
- Price or fee understanding
- Availability
- Risk
- Ease of action

### Machine view

What can the search or recommendation system interpret?

- Entities
- Topics
- Product or service attributes
- Structured data
- Links and citations
- Brand mentions
- Reviews
- Location facts
- Freshness
- Engagement
- First-hand experience
- Cross-platform consistency

A strong recommendation should connect both views to a business result.

## Step 8: Grade each finding

Use these status labels:

- **Present:** The client is visible and the result materially answers the need.
- **Partial:** The client appears, but proof, clarity, coverage, or action is weak.
- **Absent:** The client does not appear or has no meaningful asset for the moment.
- **Unknown:** Evidence is insufficient and the issue needs first-party data or testing.

Use confidence labels:

- **Confirmed:** Supported by direct observation or first-party evidence.
- **Directional:** Supported by limited or modeled evidence.
- **Hypothesis:** Requires validation.

## Step 9: Score the opportunity

The worked audit uses a 100-point model derived from:

- Demand
- Decision intent
- Visibility gap
- Feasibility
- Trust risk

The Strategy War Room uses a separate weighted score:

| Factor | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Business impact | 30% |
| Audience relevance | 25% |
| Visibility gap | 20% |
| Feasibility | 15% |
| Measurement confidence | 10% |

Do not allow a high score to replace judgment. Dependencies, compliance, and client capacity may change sequencing.

## Step 10: Convert findings into strategy

Every recommendation must state:

- Audience moment
- Search surface
- Current gap
- Action
- Why it matters
- Execution owner
- Lamark’s role
- Leading KPI
- Business KPI
- Confidence
- Dependency
- 30-day action
- 90-day action

Group final initiatives into:

- **Capture:** Win active demand.
- **Prove:** Supply independent confidence and decision evidence.
- **Connect:** Make entities, facts, locations, products, services, and messages consistent across systems.

## Reddit and community research standard

Reddit and forums provide useful audience language and objections, but they require disciplined interpretation.

- Search brand, category, comparison, problem, and review terms.
- Record the subreddit and date.
- Separate original poster claims from replies.
- Note whether the discussion is current and contextually relevant.
- Do not treat one thread as consensus.
- Do not recommend brand participation without checking community norms.
- Use limited brand-specific evidence as a monitoring finding, not as proof of broad sentiment.
- Route legal claims through legal review before publishing advice.

## AI answer research standard

For each prompt:

- Record the exact prompt.
- Record answer date and system.
- Capture brands mentioned.
- Capture cited or linked sources.
- Check fact accuracy.
- Note whether the answer resolves the need without a click.
- Identify missing entity, proof, or source signals.
- Repeat prompts over time because output can change.

## Measurement model

Separate reporting into three evidence classes:

### Measured performance

Examples: Search Console, web analytics, calls, forms, Maps actions, product revenue, app installs.

### Platform-native leading signals

Examples: YouTube search terms, view duration, saves, review volume, product-listing conversion, profile actions.

### Directional intelligence

Examples: prompt tests, community themes, creator observations, modeled audience estimates, manual result tracking.

Do not force every platform into last-click attribution.

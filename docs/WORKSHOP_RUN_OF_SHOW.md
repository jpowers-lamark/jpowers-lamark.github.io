# Search Everywhere Workshop Run of Show

## Session profile

- **Audience:** 6–8 SEO team members
- **Facilitator:** 1
- **Recommended duration:** 2 hours 15 minutes
- **Case studies:** Breezy Golf and Kanner & Pintaluga
- **Participant devices:** One laptop per participant
- **Room display:** One shared screen for the facilitator view
- **Outputs:** Audience journeys, audit evidence, opportunity scores, strategy priorities, teach-back ratings, CSV export

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the workshop, each participant should be able to:

1. Define Search Everywhere without reducing it to organic social.
2. Identify where an audience asks, compares, validates, navigates, and acts.
3. Explain why each platform plays a different role in the decision journey.
4. Separate human trust signals from machine-readable signals.
5. Run a platform-aware client audit.
6. Convert evidence into prioritized client strategy.
7. Explain Lamark’s consulting role and execution boundaries.
8. Discuss measurement limits without false precision.

## Team assignments

Split the group into two squads.

### Breezy squad

Focuses on ecommerce, lifestyle discovery, product comparison, creator influence, fit confidence, community proof, and purchase behavior.

### KP squad

Focuses on urgent legal research, local search, firm credibility, reviews, AI answers, attorney proof, and lead conversion.

Rotate participants at the midpoint so everyone sees both operating models.

## Suggested participant roles

- Audience Advocate
- Google Search Lead
- Local and Reputation Lead
- AI Search Lead
- Community and Reddit Lead
- Video and Social Search Lead
- Evidence Lead
- Client Translator

For a six-person team, combine Evidence Lead with Client Translator and combine Local with Google Search.

## Participation rules

1. Every participant owns at least one search surface during the audit.
2. Everyone contributes evidence to the shared room.
3. Every finding must state the audience need it addresses.
4. Unsupported observations are labeled as hypotheses.
5. A platform is not recommended merely because it is popular.
6. Every recommendation names an execution owner.
7. Every strategy item names a business outcome and measurement approach.
8. No one presents twice until everyone has spoken once.
9. The team may select only three major initiatives per client.
10. The room challenges weak evidence respectfully and directly.

## Stage instructions

### Stage 00: Enter the Lab

**Facilitator action**

- Create the room.
- Display the room code.
- Ask participants to join with first name and team assignment.
- Confirm that all participants appear in the roster.
- Ask participants to move their cursor so the room sees the live collaboration model.

**Opening line**

> Today is not a presentation about adding more platforms to SEO. It is a simulation of how people actually resolve a decision.

### Stage 01: The Search Fracture

**Prompt**

> Where do you search first when you want an honest answer?

Ask participants to answer as consumers, not SEO professionals. Reveal the result and ask:

- What would you check second?
- What proof would make you act?
- Which result could end the journey without a website visit?

**Teaching point**

One audience need can begin on different surfaces and continue through several more. Search Everywhere maps the full path.

### Stage 02: What Search Everywhere Means

Use the old-model versus new-model comparison.

**Definition**

> Search Everywhere is the discipline of understanding every surface an audience uses to resolve uncertainty, then advising the client how to be findable, credible, and actionable in those moments.

Clarify the Lamark boundary:

- Diagnose the search journey.
- Advise on the required signals and assets.
- Coordinate execution across SEO, content, local, PR, social, creative, UX, analytics, development, and the client.
- Do not claim ownership of organic social publishing when it is outside scope.

### Stage 03: The Search Ecosystem

Select platform nodes and discuss:

- The dominant user job on that platform.
- What the platform or system can interpret.
- Which assets work there.
- What can be measured directly.
- What remains directional.

Ask the room to identify one signal that influences more than one platform.

### Stage 04: Journey Builder

Each squad builds a path using the seven stages:

1. Trigger
2. Define
3. Generate options
4. Compare
5. Validate
6. Act
7. Return or advocate

Each journey entry must include:

- Exact query or prompt
- Platform
- Reason for the platform transition
- Evidence needed
- Failure risk

Ask teams to explain why the audience leaves one platform for the next.

### Stage 05: Client Portals

Compare Breezy and KP side by side.

**Breezy teaching lens**

- Visual discovery
- Product category and fit
- Creator influence
- Community validation
- Product detail
- Purchase and repeat behavior

**KP teaching lens**

- Urgent need definition
- Legal education
- Local legitimacy
- Reviews and credentials
- Practice-area relevance
- Contact confidence

The lesson is that Search Everywhere is an audience model, not a universal platform checklist.

### Stage 06: Audit Command Center

Assign each participant a platform or evidence role. Participants review the 55 worked findings, filter by client and surface, and add new observations.

A valid added finding includes:

- Audience segment
- Journey stage
- Platform
- Exact query or prompt
- Current brand visibility
- Evidence
- Gap
- Recommended action
- Business outcome
- Priority
- Confidence
- Execution owner

Require at least four contributions per participant across the audit and whiteboard stages.

### Stage 07: Evidence Whiteboard

Move findings onto the journey lanes. Encourage the team to create four card types:

- Evidence
- Gap
- Question
- Action

Ask:

- Where does the journey leak?
- Which stage has the least proof?
- Which finding affects several platforms?
- Which recommendation depends on another team?

### Stage 08: Signal Auction

Each squad allocates exactly 100 credits across the signal categories.

Do not let the teams change allocations after the reveal until they explain their original logic.

Ask:

- Which signal did you overvalue?
- Which signal did you underfund?
- Which investment supports several surfaces?
- Which signal is important but difficult to measure?

### Stage 09: Human + Machine View

Connect audience perceptions to system-readable signals and business outcomes.

Example:

```text
Audience concern: “Will this polo fit me?”
  → Machine-readable signal: product attributes, sizing language, customer reviews, creator demonstrations
  → Business outcome: stronger conversion and lower return risk
```

Require every participant to add at least one connection.

### Stage 10: Search Shock

Generate one shock per squad. Give teams five minutes to answer:

1. Which audience stage is affected?
2. Which surfaces are involved?
3. Is the problem visibility, trust, action, or several?
4. What should SEO recommend?
5. Who executes?
6. What changes in measurement?

### Stage 11: Strategy War Room

Teams may recommend only three major initiatives per client.

Each initiative must fit one pillar:

- **Capture:** Win active demand.
- **Prove:** Supply trust and decision evidence.
- **Connect:** Make facts and entities consistent across systems.

Use the weighted score:

- Business impact: 30%
- Audience relevance: 25%
- Visibility gap: 20%
- Feasibility: 15%
- Measurement confidence: 10%

Ask teams to defend why the fourth-ranked initiative does not move yet.

### Stage 12: Client Challenge

Generate an objection and assign a speaker. The speaker has 45 seconds.

Peer ratings cover:

- Accuracy
- Clarity
- Business relevance
- Confidence
- Lack of unnecessary jargon

Every participant speaks before anyone receives a second turn.

### Stage 13: Lock In the Model

Participants submit:

- Confidence rating
- One idea they will apply to a current client
- One behavior they will change

Export the session CSV before closing the room.

## Final facilitator close

> Search Everywhere does not ask us to own every channel. It asks us to understand the decision system well enough to advise the client where visibility, proof, and action are breaking down.
